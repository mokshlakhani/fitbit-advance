# CLAUDE.md — Project knowledge base

## 1. Overview
**DataStrap** is a self-hosted dashboard for Fitbit data. It downloads a user's data from the Google Health API (optionally plus a Google Takeout export), applies analytics ported from the open-source **OpenStrap** library (`OpenStrap/analytics-main/lib`), and serves a dark-mode dashboard on the user's own computer. No personal data is committed: everything personal lives in git-ignored files.

Metrics: recovery (0–100, with driver breakdown), day strain (0–21, Banister TRIMP), sleep (stages, efficiency, estimated sleep score), heart (resting HR, HRV, intraday HR, stress index), body (skin temperature, SpO₂, breathing rate, VO₂ max, fitness age), workouts.

## 3. Profile and time
- `profile_store.py` holds name, age, height, weight, time zone and temperature unit, filled from the Google Health `profile` and `settings` endpoints and the latest `weight`/`height` data points on every sync. Sex isn't exposed by the API; `setup.py` asks once (optional).
- All timestamps are converted with the user's IANA time zone (DST-aware). Days are keyed by local date; a night's sleep belongs to the local wake-up date.
- Max HR uses Tanaka (208 − 0.7·age), 190 if age is unknown.

## 4. Analytics

### A. Recovery (`wellness/readiness_composite.dart`)
- Each vital is compared with its own baseline: its most recent 14 readings from the previous 28 days (`BASELINE_READINGS`, `BASELINE_MAX_AGE`). With fewer than 14, that vital sits out. Robust z: `z = (v − median) / (1.4826·MAD)`, falling back to the standard deviation when the MAD is 0. Resting HR is also dropped when its whole-bpm baseline has under 1 bpm of spread.
- Orientation and weights: HRV 0.40 (higher is better), resting HR 0.30 (lower is better), breathing rate 0.20 and skin temperature 0.10 ("steady": `1.5/√(2π) − max(z,0) − 0.5·max(−z,0)`, so a rise costs twice a fall and an ordinary night averages zero). Weights are renormalised over the vitals present.
- There's no score ("Calibrating") unless at least 2 vitals with at least 0.5 of the weight qualify (OpenStrap's minimums).
- `composite_z = Σ wᵢ·oᵢ / Σ wᵢ`; score = `100 / (1 + e^(−composite_z))`, so a typical night is 50. Tiers: ≥67 optimal (strain 14–18), 34–66 adequate (10–14), <34 low (<10).
- Drivers: each vital's points are its share of (score − 50), so they add up to the score's distance from 50.

### B. Strain (`clinical/load_trimp.dart`)
- `HRR = clamp((HR − RHR)/(HRmax − RHR), 0, 1)`. TRIMP = Σ minutes with HRR ≥ 0.30 (ACSM light activity; `ACTIVE_HRR`) of HRR·a·e^(b·HRR), with Banister coefficients (men a=0.64, b=1.92; women a=0.86, b=1.67; midpoint if unknown). Sleep and sitting don't add strain.
- Strain = 21·ln(1 + TRIMP)/ln(7201).

### C. Fitness age, VO₂ max percentile, nightly stress
- VO₂ max norms: Loe et al. 2013, PLOS ONE 8:e64319 (HUNT3 Fitness Study), Table 2: mean and SD by sex and decade (20–29 … 70+). Interpolated between decade midpoints; the average of the men's and women's norms if sex is unknown.
- Percentile = normal CDF of `(VO₂ − mean)/SD` for the user's age and sex. Tiers: ≥90 elite, ≥75 excellent, ≥50 above average, ≥25 below average, else low.
- Fitness age is the age whose average VO₂ max equals the user's, limited to 25–75 (flagged `fitness_age_limit` = `lower`/`upper` at the ends).
- VO₂ max is carried forward for at most 30 days after a measurement (the API has none; it comes from Takeout, or the phone app's Takeout import).
- Nightly stress (0–100): `100 / (1 + e^(−s))` with `s = (z_RHR − z_HRV)/2` against the same 14-reading baselines. 50 is a typical night. (It isn't a Baevsky index: that needs beat-to-beat data.)

### C2. Workouts
- Each workout (Takeout `exercise-*.json` and the API's `exercise` type) is rebuilt from raw heart rate, a reading about every 2 s: Takeout `heart_rate-*.json` and the API's full-resolution files. Only readings inside the workout plus 150 s after it are kept (`workout_hr` in `src`).
- Each reading counts for the time until the next one, capped at 10 s. Results: peak HR, time-weighted average HR, time in each zone to the second, the workout's own TRIMP and strain (same formula as the day), and heart-rate recovery (the mean of the last 10 s minus the mean ±5 s around 60 s and 120 s after the end).
- Falls back to per-minute averages when a day has no raw readings (`hr_resolution`: `second` or `minute`).
- **Log +** (phone and web app home page): writes a workout to the Google Health API (`exercise` data type, `activity_and_fitness.writeonly`, asked for only on the first save), so it appears in the Fitbit app. All 181 API exercise types, labelled and grouped in `ACTIVITY_FAMILIES` (app.js). The API's `google-sources` filter hides every app's own writes, so syncs fetch DataStrap's workouts separately (`is_datastrap`: the OAuth project number or the Android package) and drop ones deleted elsewhere. Workouts carry `id` and `logged_in_app`; only those can be deleted (workout page).
- Heart-rate zones everywhere (day and workouts) follow Fitbit's Active Zone Minutes bands: light < 40%, moderate 40–59%, vigorous 60–84%, peak ≥ 85% of heart-rate reserve, with Fitbit's max HR of 220 − age (`zone_max_hr`), so they line up with the Fitbit app. Strain still uses Tanaka's max HR. Workout pages show Fitbit's own zone times (`fitbit_zones`, from the API's `heartRateZoneDurations`) when available.

### D. Sleep
- Main sleep per night, from least to most preferred: Takeout `sleep-*.json` (older staging) → Takeout `Health Fitness Data_GoogleData/UserSleeps` + `UserSleepStages` v2 sessions → Google Health API. Each night records `stage_source` (`takeout-legacy`, `takeout-v2`, `api`).
- Fitbit runs two staging algorithms side by side (`sleep_session_v1`, `v2`). The API returns v2; the export's v2 sessions match it within ~2 min per stage. Takeout's sleep JSON uses older staging (~36 min less deep, ~20 min more awake for the same night), so it's only a fallback.
- The API sometimes flags another app's session (e.g. Samsung Health via Health Connect) as `mainSleep`; ingest falls back to the night's longest Fitbit session.

### E. Sleep score estimate (`sleep_score.py`)
- Google's API doesn't provide Fitbit's sleep score, and none of the Takeout scores (v1, v2, `Sleep Score/sleep_score.csv`) match the Fitbit app. Naps aren't scored; only the main sleep.
- Model: `score = b0 + b1·hours asleep (credited up to 7 h) + b2·REM/30 min + b3·deep/30 min + b4·ln(1 + awake min)`, least squares with b1–b3 ≥ 0, b4 ≤ 0, inputs limited to the range seen in the training scores. Refit on every build from `app_sleep_scores.csv` (≥15 nights), else `DEFAULT_MODEL`.
- Reference accuracy (one user, 31 app scores, leave-one-out): ±3.0 points on average, 74% within ±5; under-scores the best nights by ~3.
- Not estimated: unstaged ("classic") nights, nights under 2 h, `takeout-legacy` nights.

### F. Body age (`body_age.py`; design and sources: `docs/BODY_AGE_DESIGN.md`)
- Physiological age from eight drivers compared with a typical peer of the same age and sex: VO₂ max, resting HR, daily steps, zone minutes, strength training, sleep length, sleep regularity (SRI) and BMI. Each published all-cause-mortality effect is converted to years with ln(HR)/γ, where γ = ln 2 / 8 (Gompertz).
- Discounts: under 30, effects from older adults count at 0.7 (VO₂ max at 1.0). Overlap groups: activity × 0.5, sleep × 0.8, resting HR × 0.6, BMI × 0.5.
- Shown with one decimal, floor 17, cap ±8 years under 30 (±10 from 30), and a ± range (model error 3 years under 30, 2 from 30).
- Needs 3 or more drivers with 14 days of data, including VO₂ max or resting HR; otherwise "Calibrating".
- Pace: the 30-day against the 180-day body age, with a 0.3-year dead band. Needs 90 days of history.
- Mirrored exactly in `mobile/src/engine.js` (`applyBodyAge`); `mobile/test/synthetic_parity.mjs` covers women, unknown sex, age 45 and pace.

## 5. Gotchas
- **Google Cloud OAuth brand rejected**: app names can't contain "Fitbit"/"Google"; leave domain/privacy-policy fields blank for testing.
- **Shared OAuth client**: must be a *Desktop app* client (any loopback port allowed). The consent screen must be **In production** for other users (Testing mode only admits listed test users and expires refresh tokens after 7 days). Unverified apps show a warning and are capped at 100 users.
- **macOS background jobs** can't read ~/Desktop, ~/Documents or ~/Downloads; `setup.py` skips scheduling there and explains why.
- **Health Connect duplicates**: the sync requests `dataSourceFamily=google-sources` to exclude other apps (Samsung Health double-counted steps and added conflicting SpO₂). Sleep can't be source-filtered at the API, so it's filtered in `api_ingest`.
- **Takeout timestamps** are UTC; files are UTC days.
