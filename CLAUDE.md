# CLAUDE.md — Project knowledge base

## 1. Overview
**DataStrap** is a self-hosted dashboard for Fitbit data. It downloads a user's data from the Google Health API (optionally plus a Google Takeout export), applies analytics ported from the open-source **OpenStrap** library (`OpenStrap/analytics-main/lib`), and serves a dark-mode dashboard on the user's own computer. No personal data is committed: everything personal lives in git-ignored files.

Metrics: recovery (0–100, with driver breakdown), day strain (0–21, Banister TRIMP), sleep (stages, efficiency, estimated sleep score), heart (resting HR, HRV, intraday HR, stress index), body (skin temperature, SpO₂, breathing rate, VO₂ max, fitness age), workouts.

## 3. Profile and time
- `profile_store.py` holds name, age, height, weight, time zone and temperature unit, filled from the Google Health `profile` and `settings` endpoints and the latest `weight`/`height` data points on every sync. Sex isn't exposed by the API; `setup.py` asks once (optional).
- All timestamps are converted with the user's IANA time zone (DST-aware). Days are keyed by local date; a night's sleep belongs to the local wake-up date.
- Max HR uses Tanaka (208 − 0.7·age), 190 if age is unknown.

## 4. Analytics

### A. Recovery (`wellness/readiness_composite.dart`)
- Each vital is compared with its own baseline: its most recent 14 readings from the previous 28 days (`BASELINE_READINGS`, `BASELINE_MAX_AGE`). With fewer than 14, that vital sits out. Robust z: `z = (v − median) / (1.4826·MAD)`, falling back to the standard deviation when the MAD is 0. Resting HR is also dropped when its whole-bpm baseline has under 1 bpm of spread.
- Orientation and weights: HRV 0.40 (higher is better), resting HR 0.30 (lower is better), breathing rate 0.20 and skin temperature 0.10 ("steady": `0.75 − max(z,0) − 0.5·max(−z,0)`, so a rise costs twice a fall and an ordinary night averages zero: 0.75·E|z|, with E|z| ≈ 1.0 for a night's robust z against a 14-reading baseline). Weights are renormalised over the vitals present.
- There's no score ("Calibrating") unless at least 2 vitals with at least 0.5 of the weight qualify (OpenStrap's minimums).
- `composite_z = Σ wᵢ·oᵢ / Σ wᵢ`; score = `100 / (1 + e^(−composite_z))`, so a typical night is 50. Tiers: ≥67 optimal (strain 14–18), 34–66 adequate (10–14), <34 low (<10).
- Drivers: each vital's points are its share of (score − 50), so they add up to the score's distance from 50.

### B. Strain (`clinical/load_trimp.dart`)
- `HRR = clamp((HR − RHR)/(HRmax − RHR), 0, 1)`. TRIMP = Σ minutes with HRR ≥ 0.30 (ACSM light activity; `ACTIVE_HRR`) of HRR·a·e^(b·HRR), with Banister coefficients (men a=0.64, b=1.92; women a=0.86, b=1.67; midpoint if unknown). Sleep and sitting don't add strain.
- Strain = 21·ln(1 + TRIMP)/ln(7201).
- Fitbit often has no resting HR for today yet: strain, zone minutes and workouts then use the most recent one from the last 7 days (`LOAD_RHR_DAYS`, saved as `strain.load_rhr`); `cardiovascular.rhr` stays empty.
- `strain.intraday_hr` holds 10-minute averages (time-aligned) for the day charts; `hr_stats` (avg/min/max) comes from the per-minute averages.

### C. Fitness age, VO₂ max percentile, nightly stress
- VO₂ max norms: Loe et al. 2013, PLOS ONE 8:e64319 (HUNT3 Fitness Study), Table 2: mean and SD by sex and decade (20–29 … 70+). Interpolated between decade midpoints; the average of the men's and women's norms if sex is unknown.
- Percentile = normal CDF of `(VO₂ − mean)/SD` for the user's age and sex. Tiers: ≥90 elite, ≥75 excellent, ≥50 above average, ≥25 below average, else low.
- Fitness age is the age whose average VO₂ max equals the user's, limited to 25–75 (flagged `fitness_age_limit` = `lower`/`upper` at the ends).
- VO₂ max is carried forward for at most 30 days after a measurement (the API has none; it comes from Takeout, or the phone app's Takeout import).
- Nightly stress (0–100): `100 / (1 + e^(−s))` with `s = (z_RHR − z_HRV)/2` against the same 14-reading baselines (z_RHR = 0 when its baseline has under 1 bpm of spread, as in recovery). 50 is a typical night. (It isn't a Baevsky index: that needs beat-to-beat data.)

### C2. Workouts
- Each workout (Takeout `exercise-*.json`, every file, and the API's `exercise` type) is rebuilt from raw heart rate, a reading about every 2 s: Takeout `heart_rate-*.json` and the API's full-resolution files. Only readings inside the workout plus 150 s after it are kept (`workout_hr` in `src`).
- Each reading counts for the time until the next one, capped at 10 s. Results: peak HR, time-weighted average HR, time in each zone to the second, the workout's own TRIMP and strain (same formula as the day), and heart-rate recovery (the mean of the last 10 s minus the mean ±5 s around 60 s and 120 s after the end).
- Falls back to per-minute averages when a day has no raw readings (`hr_resolution`: `second` or `minute`).
- **Log +** (phone and web app home page): writes a workout to the Google Health API (`exercise` data type, `activity_and_fitness.writeonly`, asked for only on the first save), so it appears in the Fitbit app. All 181 API exercise types, labelled and grouped in `ACTIVITY_FAMILIES` (app.js). The API's `google-sources` filter hides every app's own writes, so syncs fetch DataStrap's workouts separately (`is_datastrap`: the OAuth project number or the Android package) and drop ones deleted elsewhere. Workouts carry `id` and `logged_in_app`; only those can be edited (the Log + form, filled in; a `PATCH` of the data point's exercise) or deleted (workout page). Google's write scope only covers data the app added, so Fitbit-recorded workouts point to the Fitbit app.
- Heart-rate zones everywhere (day and workouts) follow Fitbit's Active Zone Minutes bands: light < 40%, moderate 40–59%, vigorous 60–84%, peak ≥ 85% of heart-rate reserve, with Fitbit's max HR of 220 − age (`zone_max_hr`), so they line up with the Fitbit app. Strain still uses Tanaka's max HR. Workout pages show Fitbit's own zone times (`fitbit_zones`, from the API's `heartRateZoneDurations`) when available.

### D. Sleep
- Main sleep per night, from least to most preferred: Takeout `sleep-*.json` (older staging) → Takeout `Health Fitness Data_GoogleData/UserSleeps` + `UserSleepStages` v2 sessions → Google Health API. Each night records `stage_source` (`takeout-legacy`, `takeout-v2`, `api`).
- Fitbit runs two staging algorithms side by side (`sleep_session_v1`, `v2`). The API returns v2; the export's v2 sessions match it within ~2 min per stage. Takeout's sleep JSON uses older staging (~36 min less deep, ~20 min more awake for the same night), so it's only a fallback.
- The API sometimes flags another app's session (e.g. Samsung Health via Health Connect) as `mainSleep`; ingest falls back to the night's longest Fitbit session.

### E. Sleep score estimate (`sleep_score.py`)
- Google's API doesn't provide Fitbit's sleep score, and none of the Takeout scores (v1, v2, `Sleep Score/sleep_score.csv`) match the Fitbit app. Naps aren't scored; only the main sleep.
- Model: `score = b0 + b1·hours asleep (credited up to 7 h) + b2·REM/30 min + b3·deep/30 min + b4·ln(1 + awake min)`, least squares with b1–b3 ≥ 0, b4 ≤ 0, inputs limited to the range seen in the training scores. Refit on every build from `app_sleep_scores.csv` (≥15 nights), else `DEFAULT_MODEL`.
- Reference accuracy (one user, 31 app scores, leave-one-out): ±3.0 points on average, 74% within ±5; under-scores the best nights by ~3.
- Not estimated: unstaged ("classic") nights, nights under 2 h, `takeout-legacy` nights.

### F. Body age (`body_age.py`; design and sources: `docs/BODY_AGE_DESIGN.md`)
- Physiological age from eight drivers compared with a typical peer of the same age and sex: VO₂ max, resting HR, daily steps, zone minutes, strength training, sleep length, sleep regularity (SRI) and BMI. Each published all-cause-mortality effect is converted to years with ln(HR)/γ, where γ = ln 2 / 8 (Gompertz).
- Discounts: under 30, effects from older adults count at 0.7 (VO₂ max at 1.0). Overlap groups: activity × 0.5, sleep × 0.8, resting HR × 0.6, BMI × 0.5.
- Shown with one decimal, floor 17, cap ±8 years under 30 (±10 from 30), and a ± range (model error 3 years under 30, 2 from 30).
- Like WHOOP Age, it moves slowly: drivers are averaged over the last 180 days, and the value and pace are worked out each Monday (`UPDATE_WEEKDAY`) and held for the week; each day's `bio_age.updated` is that Monday. A Monday needs a recovery score on 21 of the last 31 nights (`MIN_RECOVERIES`), otherwise the last good value carries on ("Calibrating" before the first).
- Needs 3 or more drivers with 14 days of data, including VO₂ max or resting HR; otherwise "Calibrating".
- Pace: 1 + (change − 0.3 y soft dead band) / years between the window centres, where change is the 30-day minus the 180-day body age; limited to −1…3. Needs 90 days of history.
- Mirrored exactly in `mobile/src/engine.js` (`applyBodyAge`); `mobile/test/synthetic_parity.mjs` covers women, unknown sex, age 45 and pace.

## 4b. Web app sign-in
- Browser OAuth gives one-hour tokens and no refresh token. `token-server/` is a Cloudflare Worker that holds the web client's secret (a Worker secret, never in the repo) and exchanges authorization codes (PKCE) and refresh tokens; it stores and logs nothing. With `tokenServer` set in `mobile/web.config.json`, `webauth.js` uses the code flow and renews in the background; without it, sign-in is refused unless `allowImplicitFlow` is set (implicit flow, silent `prompt=none` redirects).
- Bound refresh tokens: with the `SEAL_KEY` secret set, the Worker returns the refresh token AES-GCM sealed (`ds1.…`) together with the public half of a non-extractable P-256 key the browser made (`webKey` in IndexedDB). `/refresh` and `/revoke` need an ECDSA signature over `<path>.<ts>.<token>` (±5 min; on `stale_proof` the app retries on the Worker's clock). Plain tokens from older versions are sealed on their next refresh; from `REQUIRE_BOUND` (a date, 2026-11-15, or `true`) plain ones are refused. Redirects must exactly match `ALLOWED_REDIRECTS`. The Origin check is only a browser control. Tokens live in IndexedDB only, never the localStorage mirror (`store.js`).
- Deploy: `cd token-server && npx wrangler login && npx wrangler secret put GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET && openssl rand -base64 32 | npx wrangler secret put SEAL_KEY && npx wrangler deploy`.

## 4b2. Today portrait (hero)
- Today can open with a portrait of the person, with the night's vitals pinned to it (Performance: stress at the brain, SpO₂ at the throat, heart at the chest, skin temp at the shoulder; Standard: HRV, SpO₂, heart). `portraitHero` in app.js places the image by its shape inside a fixed box (0.852 wide per 1 tall) and the pins from the portrait's own points (% of the image).
- Two sources. (1) `portrait.webp` next to the app: personal, git-ignored (the repo is public), served by `serve.py` and bundled into personal Android builds, never the web build; its pin points are `FILE_PORTRAIT` in app.js. (2) One made in the phone app, kept in IndexedDB (`mobile/src/portrait.js`), which takes precedence. Without either, Today shows a faceless placeholder (`placeholder-man.webp` / `placeholder-woman.webp`, by `profile.sex`, the man's when unset; pins in `PLACEHOLDER_PORTRAITS`), with a dismissible invitation above it in an app that can make one (`ds-portrait-nudge`). A personal portrait is never shown for the sample person; the placeholder is.
- Making one: the ↻ sheet → Today portrait. The person confirms the photo is of them and consents to Gemini; the phone shrinks it to ≤1024 px JPEG (drops metadata) and posts it with their Google token to the token server's `POST /portrait` (`token-server/src/portrait.js`). That route reads the Google Health profile ID (proves a signed-in user; rate key), allows `PORTRAIT_WEEKLY_LIMIT` tries per person per week, weeks from Monday UTC (KV `PORTRAIT_LIMITS`, salted hash, 8-day TTL), checks the photo shows one person's face (`GEMINI_CHECK_MODEL`, rejections don't use a try), generates from the fixed prompt (`portraitPrompt(sex)`, `GEMINI_IMAGE_MODEL`, square): the app's own look, a high-key backlit side profile on seamless white; men bare-chested like the original, women and unset sex with bare shoulders cropped below the collarbones (never nudity); and locates the four pins (check model; defaults if that fails). Nothing is stored or logged. The phone (`cutOut` in mobile/src/portrait.js) cuts the person out of the white backdrop (edge-connected, colour distance, de-fringed), applies the hand-made portrait's toning, crops, and fits the pins to the silhouette (throat under the jaw, shoulder and chest inside the body), so it's drawn exactly like the hand-made one.
- Setup (until done, `/portrait` answers `not_configured` and nothing else changes): `cd token-server && npx wrangler secret put GEMINI_API_KEY` (paid tier), `npx wrangler secret put PORTRAIT_SALT`, `npx wrangler kv namespace create PORTRAIT_LIMITS`, add the `[[kv_namespaces]]` block to wrangler.toml, deploy. `npm test` in token-server covers the route with Google and Gemini stubbed.
- Privacy policy and terms describe it (mobile/policy).

## 4c. Standard and Performance modes (app.js)
- `state.mode` (`std` / `perf`, saved in localStorage `ds-mode`, default Standard) only decides what's shown; all data is computed in both. Switch: top of Today and the phone ↻ sheet (`window.DataStrap.setMode`).
- Standard: rings Recovery / Sleep score / Steps; day strip shows steps rings; home cards HR, resting HR, HRV, SpO₂, sleep (time, score, efficiency), steps, energy, zone minutes, workouts; tabs Today, Sleep, Activity (steps page, with workouts), Heart; Recovery page is a one-line tip (no drivers); no sleep-score breakdown, heart hourly breakdown or zone panels, trends panel, or workout strain / HR chart / zone times / HR recovery.
- Performance: everything. `PERF_ONLY` metric pages (strain, stress, skin temp, breathing, body age, VO₂ max) show a "switch to Performance" card in Standard; switching to Standard from one goes to its match (`STD_FALLBACK`) or Today.
- Switching plays `modeTakeover` (Performance: red ECG line, the page unfolds from it; Standard: a breathing orb whose light grows, then the page grows out of it). Transforms and opacity only; instant with reduced motion.

## 5. Gotchas
- **Google Cloud OAuth brand rejected**: app names can't contain "Fitbit"/"Google"; leave domain/privacy-policy fields blank for testing.
- **Shared OAuth client**: must be a *Desktop app* client (any loopback port allowed). The consent screen must be **In production** for other users (Testing mode only admits listed test users and expires refresh tokens after 7 days). Unverified apps show a warning and are capped at 100 users.
- **macOS background jobs** can't read ~/Desktop, ~/Documents or ~/Downloads; `setup.py` skips scheduling there and explains why.
- **Health Connect duplicates**: the sync requests `dataSourceFamily=google-sources` to exclude other apps (Samsung Health double-counted steps and added conflicting SpO₂). Sleep can't be source-filtered at the API, so it's filtered in `api_ingest`.
- **Takeout timestamps** are UTC; files are UTC days.
- **Privacy at rest**: health data is plain files, protected by FileVault and owner-only permissions (`private_files.lock_down()`, run by every entry point). `serve.py` refuses any Host but `127.0.0.1:<port>`/`localhost:<port>` (DNS rebinding) and sends a strict CSP. Both app builds get a CSP meta tag from `build.mjs` (own scripts only; connect-src limited to Google's APIs and the config.json addresses), so a new outside address must be added to `config.json` or the list there. The Android app has backups off (`allowBackup="false"`, `data_extraction_rules.xml`). Error text sent in feedback is scrubbed (`scrub()` in mobile.js) and shown before sending (`previewReport`, "Exactly what's added"); sync logs keep only Google's error status.
